🔍 Executive Summary

getwyld.in has a strong brand identity and a genuinely unique value proposition that is clearly communicated. The Webflow site is clean, visually distinct, and has a solid content library with 60+ blog posts covering influencer marketing, social currency, and creator lifestyle topics. The site is properly indexed, HTTPS-secure, and has basic tracking set up (GA4, Google Ads, Meta Pixel).

However, the site is leaving significant SEO value on the table. The three biggest issues are: (1) multiple H1 tags on the homepage completely undermining heading hierarchy, (2) zero structured data on high-opportunity pages like FAQs and blog posts, and (3) utility/transactional pages (checkout, paypal-checkout, success) being publicly indexed and in the sitemap. There are also noticeable page-load risks from multiple autoplay background videos and three versions of jQuery being loaded simultaneously.

Since a new website is coming, the good news is this is a clean-slate opportunity. The priority is preserving existing SEO equity through proper 301 redirects, especially for blog posts and the FAQs page — these are almost certainly your top organic traffic drivers.

Content Analysis

✅ What's Working

  • Strong blog library — 60+ posts covering influencer marketing, WYLD card tips, social currency, Shark Tank journey, creator lifestyle
  • Unique concept well-explained — The "Buy → Post → Earn" model is communicated clearly on the homepage
  • Good category structure — Blog has 5 clear categories: Marketing, Social Media, WYLD, Startup, Creators
  • FAQ page is comprehensive — Covers eligibility, WYLD Score, card usage, and getting started
  • Shark Tank content — Multiple posts around Shark Tank India Season 3 appearance — high search demand event
  • Blog search + filter — Implemented via Finsweet CMS Filter, works well
  • Newsletter subscription form — Present on blog and post pages

❌ What Needs Fixing

  • Blog posts going stale — Last blog post is from August 2024 (8+ months ago). Google rewards freshness. Publishing has stopped.
  • No related posts — Blog posts don't link to other relevant posts. Misses internal linking opportunities and increases bounce rate.
  • Some posts are very thin — Early posts like "Future of Marketing" and "Introducing WYLD" have short content. Thin content can drag down domain authority.
  • No author profiles — Posts don't show author name/photo. Hurts E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) signals.
  • No published/updated dates visible — Blog listing shows dates but they're in a small format. Important for user trust and Google freshness signals.
  • Content gap: Brand side — Very little content targeting brands looking to run influencer campaigns. The /brands page exists but is thin.
  • No comparison content — "WYLD vs. traditional cashback cards", "WYLD vs. influencer agencies" — high commercial intent, zero coverage.

Website Migration Checklist

Before you go live
🗺️

301 Redirects (Non-Negotiable)

  • Map every current URL to its new equivalent before launch
  • All /blog-post/[slug] URLs must 301 redirect — these have the most SEO equity
  • /faqs → new FAQ page (keep the URL if possible)
  • /eligibility → new eligibility page
  • /wyld-score → new WYLD score page
  • Never let a top-traffic page return 404 — that's permanent SEO loss
🚫

Pages to NOT Carry Over

  • /checkout — must be noindexed or removed from sitemap
  • /paypal-checkout — same
  • /order-confirmation — same
  • /success and /insta-success — transactional, not for Google
  • /account-deletion — utility page, not for indexing
  • /search — search results pages should be noindexed
📐

Fix in the New Site (Day 1)

  • Only ONE H1 per page — make it the primary keyword
  • Add FAQPage JSON-LD schema to the FAQ page
  • Add Article schema to all blog posts (with author, datePublished, image)
  • Add BreadcrumbList schema on inner pages
  • Fix OG tags on all blog posts (og:title, og:description, og:image)
  • Add real H2/H3 hierarchy — no animated letters as headings
⚡

Performance Improvements

  • Remove duplicate jQuery (use one version only)
  • Avoid autoplay videos as hero backgrounds — they destroy Core Web Vitals
  • Reduce Google Fonts to 2 families max, use font-display: swap
  • Aim for LCP < 2.5s and CLS < 0.1
  • Run Google PageSpeed Insights before and after launch
🔍

Post-Launch Monitoring

  • Submit new sitemap to Google Search Console immediately after launch
  • Monitor Search Console for 404 errors and coverage drops
  • Check organic traffic in GA4 for drops vs. previous period
  • Use the URL Inspection tool in Search Console to verify key pages are indexed
  • Watch for crawl errors for 30 days post-launch
📝

Content to Keep & Refresh

  • Keep all 60+ blog posts — they carry SEO equity
  • Update outdated 2022-2023 posts with fresher stats and dates
  • Restart blogging — 8+ months without a post signals stagnation to Google
  • Add author profiles to establish E-E-A-T
  • Add related posts / internal links between articles

Google Analytics Data — What to Share

Yes, please share GA data — here's exactly what would be most valuable:

To confirm which pages are actually getting traffic and which blog posts are performing, export these reports from GA4:

1. Pages & Screens report — All time or last 12 months GA4 → Reports → Engagement → Pages and screens. Export top 50 pages by Sessions. This tells us exactly which pages are driving traffic and which can be safely dropped.
2. Organic Traffic breakdown by page GA4 → Reports → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition → Filter by "Organic Search". This isolates which pages are actually found via Google (vs. direct/social). This is the most important report for the migration.
3. Landing Pages report GA4 → Reports → Engagement → Landing page. This shows the first page users land on, which directly maps to pages that are driving new visitors.
4. Search Console data (if linked to GA4) If Search Console is linked to GA4, the "Google organic search traffic" report shows which search queries are driving clicks and to which pages.
🔑 Bottom Line for the Migration

The current site has built up real SEO equity through its blog content and FAQ page. Don't let it disappear when you launch the new site. Get the 301 redirects right, add the missing schema markup from day one, and fix the H1/heading structure. Do that, and your new site will launch with the full organic authority you've earned — not reset to zero.
